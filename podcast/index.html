How it was handed to you.

Long conversations with Muslims about the inheritance they received, the way they live it now, and what they intend to hand on. Elders, reverts, imams, builders, and people whose names nobody knows.

The shape of an episode

Three questions, one guest, no rush.

Received

What was handed to you

Who taught you, what house you came up in, what you were given before you could choose any of it.

Lived

The ordinary week. The part nobody posts. What it costs, and what it has been worth.

Passed on

To children, to students, to a community — and what you hope survives you.

Why there is nothing to listen to yet

We are recording the whole season before we publish any of it.

A show that opens with one episode and then goes quiet for six weeks reads as abandoned, and it should. So we are banking a season first, editing it properly, and starting only when we can keep a rhythm you can rely on.

This is season one — the beginning of this show, not the continuation of anything. There is no back catalogue to work through, and we are not going to invent one.

You will notice there is no "follow" button on this page, and no platform badges. A follow button that leads to an empty feed is a small lie, and we would rather have the empty space than tell it. Those links appear the day there is something behind them.

If you want to know when it opens, say so and you will get exactly one email.

Guests

You do not have to be well known.

We are looking for people with something real to hand on: an elder who held a community together, a revert twenty years in, an imam who has buried and married three generations, a mother who taught six children to read.

What it involves

One recorded conversation, remote, in one sitting. We send the questions in advance because ambush is not a value of ours. You get the recording, and nothing goes out that you are unhappy with.
