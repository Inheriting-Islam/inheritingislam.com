Who this is for

Adults who were never taught, and are done pretending otherwise.

Reverts. Parents who want to keep ahead of their children. Elders who have prayed for forty years from memory and sound. Anyone in an English-first Muslim community — especially African American Muslim communities — who was handed the deen without being handed the letters.

This is not charity and it is not remedial. It is an inheritance being returned to the people it belonged to the whole time. Nobody here will make you feel late.

The path

From the letters to reading with tajwīd.

You start where you actually are, not where a syllabus assumes you are. The first conversation is about that.

  1. 01

    Qaidah — the letters and their sounds

    The Arabic letters, where each one is made in the mouth, and the short vowels. Slowly, out loud, corrected in real time by a teacher who can hear you.

  2. 02

    Qaidah — joining and reading words

    Connecting letters, long vowels, sukūn, shaddah and tanwīn — until words stop being shapes and start being words.

  3. 03

    Reading fluency

    Reading real āyāt, with the basics of where to stop and where to start again.

  4. 04

    Juz ʿAmma and tajwīd

    The thirtieth juz — the sūrahs you already stand in prayer with — read properly, with the rules of tajwīd applied rather than recited about.

The first lessons

This is where everyone starts.

Not a metaphor for beginning. The actual first three lessons of a qāʿidah, which is where the class opens whether you are nineteen or seventy.

One · the letters, by name and by sound

ابتثجحخ دذرزسشص ضطظعغفق كلمنهوي

Twenty-eight letters, each with a place in the mouth where it is made. Most of the first weeks is spent there — on the makhraj, not on memorising the chart.

Two · one letter, four shapes

بAlonebāʾ
بـOpeningba-
ـبـMiddle-ba-
ـبClosing-b

The letter that looks unfamiliar in a word is the same letter you already learned. This is the lesson that turns shapes back into reading.

Three · then the vowels

بَFatḥahba
بِKasrahbi
بُḌammahbu

Three marks, and the letters start to sound like the words you have been standing in prayer with your whole life.

Tuition

Per student, per month. Cancel whenever you need to — no term contract, no enrolment fee, no upsell waiting three lessons in.

$50 /mo

Why it is priced this low

Teaching a Muslim to read Qur'an is not a business opportunity. The fee covers the teacher's time and keeps the class serious on both sides — nothing more. The Studio pays for the rest.

And the fee is the default, not the gate. There are sponsored seats, reduced rates, and masajid who cover students from their own community. The form below has a box for exactly this — tick it and we will sort it out on the call.

Nobody here needs you to explain your finances to be taught to read.

Why a person, when we also build software

This house builds apps, and we still teach this with a human ear. Recitation has always needed one: someone who can hear that your ḥāʾ is a hāʾ and stop you on the spot.

Software can drill you, remind you, and keep your streak. It cannot correct your mouth. We are not going to pretend a microphone is a teacher in order to make this arm scale.

For masajid

Bring a class to your community.

If your masjid has adults who want to read and no one with the hours to teach them, we can run a cohort for your community — announced by you, taught by us, and yours to keep promoting. Many masajid sponsor a handful of seats for families who would not otherwise enrol.

What we ask of a host masjid

Announce it to your community, give us one point of contact, and let us know honestly what your people need. That is the whole ask — there is no fee to host.

What you get back is a cohort of adults in your own congregation who can read, and who will teach their children.

On the cost, plainly

Fifty dollars a month is what the class costs. It is not what you have to have in order to be taught.

Three reasons, and none of them is checking up on you. It tells us which communities our students come from, it lets a masjid sponsor a seat if they already do that, and if your imam knows Hamza it saves everyone a conversation.
