Inheriting Qur'an · received → passed on

Learn to read the Book of Allah, from a person.

Live classes and recordings — reading from the letters up, tajwīd, and memorising Juz ʿAmma — taught by a former imam who led taraweeh long before he built anything. Fifty dollars a month. Recommended by imams who know the teacher.

Where do I start? See the first lesson

Cohorts are being formed now. We would rather start small and teach well than open a hundred seats we cannot serve.

Who this is for

Adults who were never taught, and are done pretending otherwise.

Reverts. Parents who want to keep ahead of their children. Elders who have prayed for forty years from memory and sound. Anyone in an English-first Muslim community — especially African American Muslim communities — who was handed the deen without being handed the letters.

This is not charity and it is not remedial. It is an inheritance being returned to the people it belonged to the whole time. Nobody here will make you feel late.

The path

From the letters to reading with tajwīd.

You start where you actually are, not where a syllabus assumes you are. Two minutes of Arabic on a page will tell you which of these four you are walking into — nobody sees your answers but you.

  1. 01

    Qaidah — the letters and their sounds

    The Arabic letters, where each one is made in the mouth, and the short vowels. Slowly, out loud, corrected in real time by a teacher who can hear you.

  2. 02

    Qaidah — joining and reading words

    Connecting letters, long vowels, sukūn, shaddah and tanwīn — until words stop being shapes and start being words.

  3. 03

    Reading fluency

    Reading real āyāt, with the basics of where to stop and where to start again.

  4. 04

    Juz ʿAmma and tajwīd

    The thirtieth juz — the sūrahs you already stand in prayer with — read properly, with the rules of tajwīd applied rather than recited about.

Where it ends is as honest as where it begins. This is the reading track, and it takes you to Juz ʿAmma read correctly. Two further tracks carry on from there — see below. What none of them is is a full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ program or an ijāzah track, and if that is what you are looking for we will say so and point you somewhere better.

After the reading

Reading it and carrying it are two different things.

Finishing the reading track does not finish the work. Two further tracks run from there, and a student who already reads well starts in one of them rather than at the letters.

Tajwīd, as its own study

The rules of recitation taught properly and drilled — not recited about, but applied, with somebody listening for whether you actually do it when you are not thinking about it. A basic course to begin with; a fuller one for those who want to go further.

Whether this runs inside the reading cohorts or as a class of its own is still being decided. Either way it is taught, and either way it is included in the same fifty dollars.

Memorising Juz ʿAmma

The thirtieth juz committed to memory — the sūrahs you already stand in prayer with, carried properly rather than approximately.

This is where most people's tajwīd quietly falls apart: rules that hold while reading off a page stop holding the moment the page is gone. So the memorisation is taught with the recitation, not after it.

Neither of these is a full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ program and neither leads to an ijāzah. If that is what you want, say so and you will be pointed to someone who does it properly.

What each stage ends with

Something to put on the wall.

You were never given anything for this. Most adults who cannot read carry that quietly for decades, and finishing it deserves more than a message saying well done. Each of the three stages ends with a certificate, in Arabic and English, with your name on it in both.

Each one says what was actually demonstrated

Not "attended sixteen sessions". The wording names the thing you did in front of a teacher — read unseen text, applied the rules through a whole juz, recited it from memory. That is the only reason a certificate is worth anything.

Every one carries a number that can be checked at the register, and states plainly that it is not an ijāzah and confers no licence to transmit.

Shown blank, on purpose. These are unfilled specimens and there is no download — a certificate you can download is a certificate anyone can put their own name on.

And none have been awarded yet, because the first cohorts are still forming. The register is empty and says so.

Tuition

Per student, per month. Cancel whenever you need to — no term contract, no enrolment fee, no upsell waiting three lessons in.

$50 /mo

Why it is priced this low

Teaching a Muslim to read Qur'an is not a business opportunity. The fee covers the teacher's time and keeps the class serious on both sides — nothing more. The Studio pays for the rest.

And the fee is the default, not the gate. There are sponsored seats, reduced rates, and masajid who cover students from their own community. The form below has a box for exactly this — tick it and we will sort it out on the call.

Nobody here needs you to explain your finances to be taught to read.

Why a person, when we also build software

This house builds apps, and we still teach this with a human ear. Recitation has always needed one: someone who can hear that your ḥāʾ is a hāʾ and stop you on the spot.

Software can drill you, remind you, and keep your streak. It cannot correct your mouth. We are not going to pretend a microphone is a teacher in order to make this arm scale.

Private seats

One to one, for the few who need it.

Some students cannot make a fixed weekly hour, some are further along than any cohort forming right now, and some simply learn faster with nobody else in the room. For them there are a small number of private seats.

This is expensive on purpose, and the reason is worth saying plainly. The group class is fifty dollars and it is the same teacher, the same syllabus and the same correction. What the private rate buys is not better teaching — it is an hour that cannot be given to anyone else.

It is also what pays for the sponsored seats. The full-rate work carries the khidma work here exactly as it does everywhere else in this house.

Weekly, one to one

A standing forty-five minute session each week, at an hour agreed with you. Recorded, like everything else, so you can go over it again.

$320 /mo

A single session

One forty-five minute session, not repeating — to be placed properly, to fix one thing, or to see whether this is for you at all.

$90 once

Three at a time, and that is the real number

Hamza teaches around a full-time job and everything else in this house. Three standing private students is what can be done properly rather than what could be sold.

When they are taken they are taken, and you will be told that rather than added to a list quietly. If a cohort would serve you just as well, we will say so — that is two hundred and seventy dollars a month we are talking you out of, and it is still the right advice.

For masajid

Bring a class to your community.

If your masjid has adults who want to read and no one with the hours to teach them, we can run a cohort for your community — announced by you, taught by us, and yours to keep promoting. There is no fee to host, and many masajid sponsor a handful of seats for families who would not otherwise enrol.

What hosting involves

What we ask of a host masjid

Announce it to your community, give us one point of contact, and let us know honestly what your people need. That is the whole ask — there is no fee to host.

What you get back is a cohort of adults in your own congregation who can read, and who will teach their children.

On the cost, plainly
